# Canvas LMS (Instructure): Research

Subject: Canvas LMS by Instructure, the most-praised LMS in the sources reviewed. Purpose: derive user-story input for Scientia, a minimal replacement for Blackboard used by students and teachers.

Research date: 2026-10-09. Sources are cited inline by number; full URLs are in Sources. Claims without a verified source are marked (unverified).

## Summary

- Canvas is the most-praised LMS in these sources: G2 lists it at 4.5/5 across 1,839 reviews [S2], and Capterra reviewers praise its ease of use [S1].
- Its preferred UX rests on one place for assignments, grades and materials, a to-do list and calendar, and a clean announcement flow [S1, S6, S3].
- Its main frustrations are notification overload, a gradebook that fits poorly and can disagree with other tools, inconsistent course layouts set by each instructor, weaker mobile use, and slowness [S1, S4, S11].
- Trust is now a pain point: a May 2026 breach and a multi-day outage during finals [S8, S9], followed by a call for a state legislative audit [S19].
- Caveat: pain-point evidence comes from a small, mixed sample of reviews and aggregators, not a systematic survey. Canvas's own VPAT could not be read, so its WCAG level is unverified.

## Features by area

**Courses & content**
- Modules with prerequisites, completion requirements and date-based drip releases; Blueprint Courses let admins push master-course changes to linked courses; Course Import and Canvas Commons for reuse [S3].
- Pages, assignments, discussions, quizzes, groups and multimedia lessons, with differentiated paths and reusable content [S6].
- Easy transfer of courses from year to year [S1].

**Announcements**
- Announcements reach the whole course and trigger notifications [S3]. Reviewers call the feature "a game changer" [S1].

**Assignments & submission**
- Submission types: online text, file upload, on paper [S3]. Assignment groups support weighted grading [S3].
- Reviewers report that creating new assignments can be hard [S1].

**Grading & feedback**
- SpeedGrader shows the submission, rubric, grade field and feedback tools on one screen, with PDF annotation and video feedback [S3]. Rubrics can align to learning outcomes [S3].
- Annotations, peer review and several feedback formats are offered [S6].

**Gradebook**
- Weighting, dropping the lowest score, and controlled grade release [S3].
- Reviewers say the gradebook "assumes you're running a traditional classroom" and once erased entries [S1].

**Calendar & due dates**
- A built-in calendar and dashboard track upcoming work [S7]. A student calendar shows all assignments in one place, plus a sidebar to-do list [S21].
- Users ask on Reddit whether To Do items are shared with classmates [S13]. Sharing behaviour is unverified in the sources read.

**Discussions**
- Threaded replies, instructor prompts, graded participation and structured peer review [S3]. Other sources call the tools clunky and formal [S3].
- Instructors report more clicks, harder thread-following and a cluttered split-screen view after a redesign [S17].

**Messaging**
- An Inbox for internal messaging and course chat for quick questions [S3]. Students can message teachers through the Inbox, with contact settings that control notifications [S21].

**Quizzes**
- Time limits, shuffled answers, question banks, several question types, and a Student Analysis Report showing time per question and commonly missed items [S3].
- Reviewers call quiz creation time-consuming [S1]. Students report Lockdown Browser problems [S13].

**Groups**
- Groups are listed as a course-building element [S6]. Depth of group features is unverified.

**Attendance**
- Unknown. Not covered in any source read.

**Notifications**
- Push and email notifications can be customised by type on web and mobile [S3]. Students can open the account menu, select notifications and choose what to receive [S10].

**Mobile**
- Three free apps (student, teacher, parent). The student app covers materials, submissions, grades, messaging and quizzes, and works with screen readers. The teacher app offers mobile SpeedGrader. The parent app is view-only [S3].
- Quizzes need a stable connection, and the platform is largely unusable offline [S3].

**Accessibility**
- Canvas commits to W3C WAI and Section 508, tests each feature manually with JAWS, NVDA, VoiceOver and TalkBack, uses ARIA landmarks and headings, offers "move-to" as a keyboard alternative to drag and drop, and includes an accessibility checker in the Rich Content Editor plus UDOIT for course scans [S5].
- Known limits: only listed screen-reader and browser pairs are tested; third-party LTI tools may not meet Canvas standards [S5].
- The student iOS app is rated "very accessible" by an AppleVis reviewer, with minor remaining issues [S14].
- The VPAT's WCAG version and level are unverified; the VPAT PDF could not be read [S4].

**Integrations**
- App Center with 1,000+ LTI tools, a REST API, no-code automation through Zapier or Make.com, SSO, Turnitin [S3]. SIS and Clever SSO and Google Classroom connections [S6].
- Grade pass-back to the SIS has had sync issues [S1]. Some internal tools do not sync well with third-party apps [S3].

**Roles & admin**
- District course templates, subaccounts, roles, analytics and data services; family access and a K-5 experience [S6].
- Reviewers call administration substantial and say advanced admin functions are hard for new users [S6, S1].

## Pain points

1. **Inconsistent course layouts and too many clicks.** Each instructor's layout varies, and materials are hard to find. Reviewer: "inconsistency in the design and layout between different courses" [S1].
2. **Notification overload and buried settings.** Reviewers report missed alerts, overwhelming volume and hard-to-find settings [S1]. One high-school student received about 55 emails a day for one class [S10].
3. **Gradebook fit and reliability.** The gradebook assumes a traditional classroom, may erase entries, and has pass-back sync issues [S1]. Students at Temple could not tell whether they were passing because grades were not kept current [S20].
4. **Mobile is weaker than desktop.** Reviewers prefer the browser for some tasks and report app/browser mismatch [S1]. The mobile app is described as sometimes slow and buggy [S3].
5. **Slowness and bugs.** Pages hang or need reloads, and submissions sometimes go unrecognised [S1]. Login timeouts and server gateway errors appear in Trustpilot reviews [S12].
6. **Outages and security trust.** A May 2026 breach led to a multi-day outage during finals; Instructure later apologised for lack of transparency [S8, S9]. A state senator called for an audit over concentrating student records in one platform [S19].
7. **Discussions are hard to follow.** Reported as more clicks, weaker thread-following and lower participation after a redesign [S17].
8. **Dated look, heavy setup, limited customisation.** Reviewers say the UI "needs a refresh", that setup is "a heavy lift", and that background colours cannot be changed [S1]. Implementation can take about four months [S2].
9. **Support depends on the institution.** Reviewers report variable response times, and one says support "refuses to help unless the teacher files an IT ticket" [S1, S12].
10. **Cost and add-ons.** Reviewers say base-price features require paid add-ons; one institution (Emporia State) pushed back on fee increases and moved for ease of use, not savings [S3, S15].
11. **Reddit recurring questions.** Finding all assignments (24 posts), discussion replies not showing (8), and calendar sharing confusion (4) are among the most common flairs [S13]. This is a flair count from an aggregator, not a systematic sample.

## Worth copying

- One place for assignments, grades and materials, plus a to-do list and due-date calendar [S1, S21].
- Announcements that reach the whole course with notifications [S3].
- SpeedGrader-style grading: submission, rubric and feedback on one screen [S3].
- Teacher control over module navigation, which reviewers praise [S1].
- Per-type notification controls [S3].
- Quiz analysis showing where students struggled [S3].
- A documented accessibility process: named screen-reader testing, an in-editor checker, keyboard alternatives [S5].
- Course reuse from year to year [S1].

## Worth avoiding

- Layouts that vary by instructor with no shared structure [S1].
- Notifications sent for everything, with settings buried [S10, S1].
- A gradebook that depends on each teacher updating it, leaving students to recalculate [S20].
- A mobile app that is a reduced web view, with no offline use [S3, S1].
- A single platform that holds all student records without a clear incident-response story [S8, S9].
- Discussion threads that take many clicks to follow [S17].
- Features gated behind paid add-ons [S3].
