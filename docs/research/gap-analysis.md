# Gap analysis: what Blackboard Learn (Ultra) and Canvas offer that Scientia does not yet

Research date: 2026-10-09. Scope: functional areas for students, teachers and admins. This builds on [blackboard.md](blackboard.md), [canvas.md](canvas.md) and [synthesis.md](synthesis.md). Items the synthesis already parked under "Later" are repeated here only so they can be ranked against everything else, with new evidence added. Competitor names appear only in this research file.

## How to read this

- **Scientia today** means the 43 stories in `docs/stories/` and the 29 routes in `docs/design/ia.md`. Where a gap depends on something the stories do not say (for example "one file per submission"), the cell says "(inferred from stories)". I did not read app code.
- **Priority** is for a school replacing either product: Must have (a school cannot switch without it), Should have (staff will complain loudly without it), Nice to have.
- **Evidence** links are to university help desks and vendor community pages that describe the feature. The official Blackboard help site (help.blackboard.com) now redirects to help.anthology.com and only its index loaded for me, so most Blackboard detail comes from university help desks that document the same features. Several Canvas Community articles redirected or loaded without body text; where that happened I used a university guide instead and say so.
- A statement marked "(unverified)" could not be confirmed from a page I opened.

## What Scientia already covers (no gap)

Course modules with pages, files and links and explicit publish; announcements with pin and read state; assignments (file and/or text, one due date, late allowed or closed, resubmit until grading starts); grading queue with draft versus released grades and withdraw; gradebook grid with totals and CSV export; student dashboard with upcoming and missing work; month calendar; threaded discussions with teacher delete; five in-app notification kinds with switches; roster by email; admin users and courses; WCAG-focused build; responsive web. Sources for the competitor side of these are in the earlier research files.

## Top 10, with a short justification each

1. **Quizzes and question banks.** Every school uses auto-marked quizzes, and both products have them. Without one, teachers keep a second tool open all term. A narrow version (five question types, three auto-marked) covers most of the use.
2. **Weighted grades, drop lowest and letter bands.** Most courses are graded by "tests 40%, coursework 60%". A single flat percentage (US-12) cannot show the course result a school must record.
3. **Rubrics.** Teachers use them to grade faster and more consistently, and students rely on them to see why they lost points. Staff ask for better rubrics more than almost anything else in the survey that was available.
4. **Per-student due dates, extensions and accommodations.** Schools are legally and morally required to honour agreed extra time. Today an accommodated student would show as Late.
5. **Course copy and reuse each term.** Teachers rebuild the same course every term otherwise. It is also where the incumbents fail, so a safe version is a real advantage.
6. **Marks without a submission, excused work, grade import.** Class tests, participation and paper work need a place in the gradebook. Without it the gradebook is not the single record the design promises.
7. **Bulk import and course management after creation.** A school cannot create hundreds of accounts one by one, and cannot rename, hand over or archive a course. This blocks any real roll-out.
8. **Email notifications (one daily summary) and password reset by email.** People who leave the app never see in-app alerts, and admins cannot hand-reset every forgotten password. A single digest avoids the 55-emails-a-day trap.
9. **More than one teacher or an assistant per course.** Co-teaching and teaching assistants are normal. At the moment only one person can run a course.
10. **Groups and group assignments.** Team projects are common, and both products offer them. A team-submission option is the smallest change that makes the feature useful.

Next in line (ranks 11 to 14): private messaging attached to work, sections, single sign-on, and file preview with marking on submissions.

## Notes and limits

- Priority and rank are my judgement from the sources above; they are not survey results. No Reddit threads were retrievable, and the evidence for Blackboard pain points is mostly institutional (surveys, vendor issue lists), as in [blackboard.md](blackboard.md).
- Items marked "title only" are search results whose pages I did not open, so only the existence of the page is established.
- Ranks 20 to 37 are mainly "possible later" items. Rows 23, 29 and 30 depend on integrations the project has excluded for now.
- Localisation, trash and undo, and notification digests per course are already in the "Later" list of [synthesis.md](synthesis.md) and I found no new evidence to re-rank them.
